What are Cyber Attacks?

Cyber attacks are attempts to damage, steal, or disrupt data and systems. They target individuals, businesses, and governments using digital means, often exploiting software flaws or user mistakes.

Brief History

Cyber attacks date back to the 1980s. Early examples include computer worms and viruses. Today’s attacks are more complex, involving ransomware, phishing, and large-scale data breaches.

Types of Cyber Attacks

  • Phishing: Fake emails or messages that trick users into giving up personal info or clicking malicious links.
  • Ransomware: Malware that locks your data and demands payment to unlock it.
  • DDoS Attacks: Flooding a website or server with traffic to crash it.
  • Man-in-the-Middle (MitM): Intercepting communication between two parties, often on unsecured Wi-Fi.
  • Malware: Software designed to damage or steal data, like viruses or spyware.
  • SQL Injection: Inserting malicious code into a database query to access or alter data.
  • Password Attacks: Attempts to crack or steal login credentials.
  • Insider Threats: Employees or trusted users intentionally or accidentally causing harm.

How to Stay Protected

  • Use strong, unique passwords and consider a password manager.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication where possible.
  • Update software regularly to fix security flaws.
  • Be cautious with emails—don’t click on suspicious links or attachments.
  • Backup important data to recover from ransomware or data loss.
  • Use antivirus and firewall tools to add extra layers of defense.
  • Avoid public Wi-Fi for sensitive activities unless using a VPN.
