What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyber attack that uses disguised email as a weapon. The goal is to trick the email recipient into believing that the message is something they want or need — like a request from their bank or a note from someone in their company — and to click a link or download an attachment.

History of Phishing

The term "phishing" originated in the 1990s when hackers used fake AOL accounts to steal passwords. The term is a play on the word "fishing," as attackers are "fishing" for sensitive information.

Common Phishing Tactics

  • Email Phishing: The most common form, where attackers send fraudulent emails that appear to be from legitimate sources.
  • SMS Phishing (Smishing): Phishing attempts via text messages, often containing links to malicious websites.
  • Voice Phishing (Vishing): Phone calls pretending to be from legitimate organizations, asking for sensitive information.
  • Clone Phishing: A legitimate email is taken and modified to include a malicious link or attachment, then sent to the original recipient.

How to Recognize Phishing Attempts

  • Check the sender's email address for misspellings or unusual domains.
  • Look for generic greetings like "Dear Customer" instead of your name.
  • Be cautious of urgent language that pressures you to act quickly.
  • Hover over links to see the actual URL before clicking.
  • Be wary of unexpected attachments or links.

Tips for Protection

  • Use two-factor authentication (2FA) whenever possible.
  • Keep your software and antivirus programs up to date.
  • Educate yourself and others about phishing tactics.
  • Report phishing attempts to your email provider or IT department.
  • Trust your instincts; if something seems off, it probably is.
